\begin{abstract}
    The random-oracle-based Fiat--Shamir transform is fundamental to constructing practical signature schemes. It is uninstantiable in general (Goldwasser and Kalai, FOCS 2003). We ask whether the most practical instances of the paradigm can be instantiated and go on to give standard-model for Schnorr-signatures and a variant of Dilithium's. All our results are under non-interactive assumpmtions.

   \quad\quad To instantiate Schnorr signatures, we first define a notion of tunable lossy functions, which can be realized through a variety of different assumptions such as DDH and LWE. Our instantiation is a tunable lossy function composed with a collision resistant hash function. Here we give a standard-model proof of key-recovery under chosen-message-attack. Intuitively, the proof replaces random-oracle programming by rejection sampling over the enumerated image. 

    \quad\quad To target full unforgeability, we instantiate the hash function mainly using indisguishability obfscation and any function meeting the circular discrete-logarithm assumption of Cho, Fuchsbauer, O'Neill and Sefranek (CRYPTO 2025). Here  we give a standard-model proof of static-message unforgeability, where the forger declares its signing and forgery messages before seeing the verification key, and for a \emph{a priori} polynomially-bounded number of signatures fixed at key generation. We show that by ``salting'' Schnorr signature our techniques enable  proving full-fleged EUF-CMA security. 

   \quad\quad Finally, we extend the program to lattice-based schemes and give analogous results there.

    \keywords{Fiat--Shamir signatures \and Schnorr \and Dilithium \and random-oracle instantiation \and lossy functions \and obfuscation}
\end{abstract}
