\begin{abstract}
    The random-oracle-based Fiat--Shamir transform is fundamental to constructing practical signature schemes, and it is uninstantiable in general (Goldwasser and Kalai, FOCS 2003). We ask whether the most widely deployed instances of the paradigm can be instantiated, and we give standard-model security results for Schnorr signatures and for a variant of Dilithium. All our results are under non-interactive assumptions.

   \quad\quad To instantiate Schnorr signatures, we first define a notion of tunable lossy functions, realizable from assumptions such as DDH, with a candidate from LWE. Our instantiation is a tunable lossy function composed with a collision-resistant hash function, and we prove key-recovery security under chosen-message attack, assuming DL is hard in the Schnorr group. Intuitively, the proof replaces random-oracle programming by rejection sampling over the enumerated lossy image. 

    \quad\quad To target full unforgeability, we instantiate the hash function using indistinguishability obfuscation and any conversion function meeting the circular discrete-logarithm assumption of Cho, Fuchsbauer, O'Neill, and Sefranek (CRYPTO 2025). Here we prove static-message unforgeability, where the forger declares its signing and forgery messages before seeing the verification key. The guarantee is bounded: for each polynomial $Q$ there is a scheme secure against forgers declaring at most $Q$ messages, since $Q$ fixes the size to which the hash circuit is padded. We then show that ``salting'' Schnorr signatures lets our techniques prove full-fledged EUF-CMA security, with the message entering the hash through the tunable lossy function and with quasi-polynomially secure versions of the same primitives. 

   \quad\quad Finally, we extend the program to lattice-based Fiat--Shamir with aborts, the paradigm of Lyubashevsky signatures that Dilithium instantiates, and give analogous key-recovery and static-message unforgeability results. The circular problem there is a SIS analogue of CDL, and for our conversion function it reduces tightly to plain SIS, so the assumptions are SIS, LWE, iO, and puncturable PRFs.

    \keywords{Fiat--Shamir signatures \and Schnorr \and Dilithium \and random-oracle instantiation \and lossy functions \and obfuscation}
\end{abstract}
