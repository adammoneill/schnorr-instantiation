\begin{abstract}
    Almost all positive security results for the widely used Schnorr signature scheme are in idealized models. The exception is a standard-model result of Paillier and Vergnaud (ASIACRYPT 2005), who prove key-recovery security for any hash function under the interactive OMDL assumption. We give standard-model instantiations under non-interactive assumptions, including, to our knowledge, the first to achieve any form of existential unforgeability for Schnorr.

    For key recovery, we define tunable lossy functions. Their lossy image bound is a parameter, and our reduction gives an explicit tradeoff among mode indistinguishability, image-enumeration time, and DL security. The proof replaces random-oracle programming by rejection sampling over the enumerated image. Instantiating the construction with Zhandry's strongly efficiently enumerable ELFs gives key-recovery security from ordinary DL in the Schnorr group, exponential DDH in auxiliary groups, and collision-resistant hashing.

    For unforgeability, we instantiate the hash function with an obfuscated circuit built from puncturable PRFs and a conversion function for the circular discrete-logarithm problem. Polynomially secure CDL, iO, and puncturable PRFs give static-message unforgeability, where the forger declares its signing and forgery messages before seeing the verification key. For a salted variant of Schnorr in which the message enters the hash through a tunable lossy function in injective mode, we prove adaptive EUF-CMA security for messages of any polynomial length. The reduction switches the pre-hash to lossy mode and guesses hash digests from its enumerable image, so the guessing losses are bounded by the image size rather than by the message space. With a quasi-polynomial image bound, quasi-polynomially secure CDL, iO, and puncturable PRFs suffice. The signer samples both its nonce and its salt uniformly and independently.

    Finally, we extend the program to lattice-based Fiat--Shamir with aborts. For an uncompressed Dilithium-shaped scheme over a power-of-two modulus with gadget challenges, we prove key-recovery security from search LWE and the same lossy hash family, with no obfuscation and every ingredient lattice-based. We then prove static-message unforgeability from SIS, LWE, iO, and puncturable PRFs, all polynomially secure at a quasi-polynomial modulus. The circular problem here is a SIS analogue of CDL, and for the gadget conversion function it reduces tightly to plain SIS.

    \keywords{Schnorr signatures \and Dilithium \and random-oracle instantiation \and lossy functions \and obfuscation}
\end{abstract}
